# Shots

Show the user the shot plan before writing prompts, and confirm it.

## Shot count
- No limit if the user asks for more; otherwise keep it as few as possible (one or two shots
  is usually best for 5 to 15 seconds).
- Every cut must be smooth. Avoid jump cuts: change shot size or angle clearly between shots.

## Camera words (use these widely understood terms)
- **Shot size:** wide, full, medium, medium close-up, close-up, extreme close-up.
- **Angle:** eye level, low angle, high angle, top-down, three-quarter, side profile.
- **Moves:** static, slow push-in, pull-out, pan, tilt, truck left or right, pedestal up or down,
  orbit, tracking, crane.
- **Focus:** shallow depth of field, deep focus, rack focus.

## Moves
| Prefer | Use with care | Avoid unless asked |
|---|---|---|
| static, slow push-in, slow orbit (up to about 90°), truck, pedestal | pan, tilt, pull-out, tracking, crane, rack focus | whip pan, crash zoom, handheld shake, dolly zoom, fast moves |
- One camera move per shot; moves ease in and out.
- Keep motion slow and smooth so every variation stays in sync.

## Framing
- The thing that changes is visible for at least 40 to 50% of the video, large and sharp.
- For a product swap, keep the product big (about a quarter of the frame or more) and never
  covered by hands. For a person swap, keep the face visible. For a scene swap, show plenty of it.
- Leave room for the design: keep the area for the headline, button and thumbnail strip clear
  of the product and faces (see the design skill for each size).

## Before building
Check the plan: nothing important will be covered by text or thumbnails, nothing gets cropped,
and the swap reads clearly. Fix it, keep it on brand, and tell the user if something you'd
agreed earlier has to change.
