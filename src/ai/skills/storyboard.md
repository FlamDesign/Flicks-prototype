# The storyboard

The storyboard is the approval before any video credits are spent (propose_plan). The user sees
**frames and directions only**: Variation 1 as 3 or 4 frames (what happens, how it's framed; the app
draws a quick still per frame) and one direction line per variation. No layout, no copy, no links on
the card: the layout comes after Variation 1 (build skill). Call it "the storyboard", never "the plan".
Prompts are never shown or quoted; describe the idea.

- One line comes with it: the concept's name and what the first video shows.
- The footer names the whole cost: the first video now, the others once it's approved.
- When the user edits a direction, it reaches the video as their direction.

## By intent
- **Make:** Variation 1 is a new video from the references (references skill); each variation's
  direction says only its change.
- **Change:** no concepts. If the user's video is a variation, it is Variation 1 as it is (video_id,
  nothing made, no cost) and every other variation is an edit of it; its storyboard shows the edits
  as stills drawn from the video's first frame. If the video is only the look to follow, Variation 1
  is made from it as a reference video and the storyboard shows it as frames.
- **Assemble:** propose_plan with every variation's video_id and no prompts; nothing is generated.
  The app checks the clips match in shape and length and tells you if they don't: say it plainly and
  ask how to go on (replace the odd one, or go on anyway).

## Directing (make, and a change that follows a look)
You're the director: energy, camera, moves, cuts and pacing are your call, calm or bold, one
continuous shot or several. Keep in mind:
- Something happens from the first frame; a static video rarely makes people tap.
- The swap is in the first frame, clearly visible 40 to 50% of the time, and in a hero moment near
  the end.
- No shaky handheld motion, no accidental jump cuts.
- Variations are edits of Variation 1, so one thing changes, with the same shape and size.
- The swap and faces stay out of the clear zones the layout will use (formats skill).

## Camera words (use them in directions and prompts)
Size: wide, full, medium, medium close-up, close-up, extreme close-up (macro). Angle: eye level, low,
high, top-down, three-quarter, side profile. Move: static, slow push-in, pull-out, pan, tilt, truck,
pedestal, orbit, tracking, crane. Focus: shallow, deep, rack focus.

## Before you send it
Check: something happens, the swap reads, nothing important sits where text or thumbnails will go,
nothing gets cropped. If something agreed has to change, say so in the line that comes with the card.
