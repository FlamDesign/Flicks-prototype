# What makes a Flick great

A Flick is one video with 2 to 5 versions inside. Viewers tap thumbnails to switch, and tap a
button to act. It wins when switching is irresistible and the choice is obvious.

## The five tests (a great Flick passes all of them)
1. **One idea.** One product or offer, one scene, one action. If you need "and" to describe it, cut.
2. **Visible difference.** Every variation must look different in a thumbnail-sized glance: colour,
   product, person, scene or price. If two variations look alike at 60 px, merge or change one.
3. **Same stage.** Everything else stays identical across variations: framing, camera move, timing,
   light, layout. Only the thing that varies changes. This is what makes switching feel magical.
4. **Read in 2 seconds.** Headline of 2 to 6 words, one price, one button. Nothing to decipher.
5. **One clear action.** One button, a verb, linked per variation to the exact product.

## Defaults when unsure
- 3 variations (enough to invite switching, few enough to compare).
- Start on the most popular or best-looking variation.
- Product close to camera, slow motion (push-in, orbit or a short reveal), clean background.
- Text low or high, never over the product or a face.
- Thumbnails show the product itself, square, consistent style.

## Never
- Variations that differ only in text.
- More than one button or competing calls to action.
- Long copy, paragraphs, hashtags, emoji in headlines.
- Words Flam never uses: "get used", "can enter", "reach into", "immersive", "AR", "VR", "MR".
